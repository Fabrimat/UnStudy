Entropy and the second law

The chapter builds the second law out of one observation: heat flows from hot to cold by itself, never the other way. Entropy is introduced as the quantity that makes this one-way flow measurable.

Key points

  • For a reversible process, the entropy change equals the heat exchanged divided by the temperature.
  • In an isolated system entropy never decreases, which rules out a perpetual motion machine of the second kind.
  • The Carnot cycle sets the upper limit on the efficiency of any heat engine working between two temperatures.

Worked examples

Two examples close the chapter: mixing two bodies at different temperatures, and the free expansion of an ideal gas. Both show entropy rising even though no heat enters or leaves the system.
